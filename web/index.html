For educators

Everything in the railway is connected. Do you teach it that way?

Timetabling in one course, signalling in the next, freight in another term. ARail puts them all on the same railway, so students see what their decision does to everyone else.

  1. In the lab

    Run it live

    Real trains, a virtual town, students in the roles: signaller, operations controller, asset manager.

  2. In the lecture

    Show it

    Cases to click through and clips for your slides: a train breaks down, a signal fails, a connection is lost.

  3. At home

    Let them play

    Missions on your subject. Students collect stars; you get a class that has already met the problem.

One infrastructure across all your courses: that is where cross-disciplinary thinking starts.

For scientists

Your method works in isolation. Does it survive the whole railway?

A clean model meets passengers who miss their connection, a unit that breaks down and a signal that fails at rush hour. Find out before the reviewers ask.

  1. Implement

    Your paper as a plugin

    A simulation, a dispatching rule, a demand model: one ES module, no build step.

  2. Stress

    Meet the rest of the system

    Town, timetable, fleet, track and disruptions already run. Your method runs among them.

  3. Show

    A link instead of a figure

    Colleagues and reviewers open your method in the browser and watch it work, on a model railway.

Methods that others can open, rerun and compare, on the same infrastructure.

For practitioners

A disruption never stays in one department. Does your training?

Signal box, control centre, workshop, customer information: everyone knows their own desk. ARail shows the whole railway on a bad day, and lets people sit at the desk next door.

  1. Rehearse

    Replay the bad day

    An incident as a scenario: run it, change one decision, run it again.

  2. Onboard

    Sit at the desk next door

    Role games for new colleagues and old hands: a signaller's half hour, an operations controller's morning, an asset manager's budget year.

  3. Explain

    Show it, don't slide it

    For clients, authorities and the public: what a closure, a spare unit or a budget cut does to passengers.

A shared picture of the system, before the next disruption draws it for you.

What runs today

Six prototypes, one railway

Each opens in the app, on a photo of the lab. They work the same on your layout and on the live camera.

A town that goes to work

Residents walk, take the bus and board trains through the doors, on a fast clock through day and night. Their colour shows their mood.

Teach: station and public transport planning, intermodal trips, demand over the day.

Research: demand models, transfer behaviour, crowding on platforms.

Practice: what a new stop or a changed timetable does to a town.

Open this example Day and night Streets and buses

A working container terminal

Trains, trucks and barges, a gantry crane and a reach stacker. Pick a container and its new place, and the crane moves it. Real wagons carry virtual containers.

Teach: freight transport, terminal operation, the hand-over between modes.

Research: stacking and crane strategies, loading plans for a train.

Practice: the terminal explained to new staff, customers and visitors.

Open this example Hybrid train Virtual terminal Guide

A train breaks down

RB 31 reports a fault at the platform. A spare unit comes from the depot, the passengers change trains, the broken unit runs to the workshop: what it costs. Or plan a normal morning and run it.

Teach: disruption management, spare units, maintenance (ECM), penalties; vehicle and crew scheduling.

Research: rescheduling and dispatching rules, vehicle and crew plans under disruption.

Practice: a disruption drill for the control centre; what a spare unit is worth.

Open this example Plan a morning The game Guide

A signal fails, a track wears out

Exit signal N2 fails: the signaller closes its track, the repair team drives out and exchanges it. Then worn track G3 is closed and renewed with a construction train.

Teach: maintenance and renewal, track closures, the condition of assets and their age.

Research: maintenance strategies, planning of closures, models of wear.

Practice: a closure explained to operators and clients; budget choices made visible.

Open this example The game Guide

A connection is lost

Ada's train is 20 minutes late and her connection is gone. Her travel app finds a bus; she takes it while the late train comes in without her. Then her journey in numbers, and her rights.

Teach: passenger information, connections and how they break, passenger rights.

Research: passenger information, holding connections, route choice.

Practice: a delay as the customer lives it, for customer information training.

Open this example The game Guide

A bouquet of disruptions

Delays, cancellations, closures, signal failures, crowds and replacement buses, started by hand or scripted: rush hour with a signal failure, the crowd after the match.

Teach: dispatching, passenger information, exercises that can be repeated.

Research: scripted scenarios as a test bench: the same disruption, another strategy.

Practice: incident reviews and drills, scripted and repeatable.

Bring it to your lab

Stickers, a phone, a browser

Digital twins are boring. We built a sister: an image of the layout that is laid over it, and technically independent of it. Any scale: H0, TT, N.

  1. 1StickArUco or AprilTag markers, 30 mm, one every 30–40 cm. Print them from the marker sheets.
  2. 2FilmOne slow video of the table. arail-survey measures every marker and writes the layout and an orthophoto.
  3. 3BuildPlatforms, streets, houses, a terminal: in the flyover (F), on a grid, also beyond the real table.
  4. 4Connectarail-bridge brings the positions of the real trains from your control system, or from the built-in simulator.

For developers: plugins

No build step, no framework. A plugin is an ES module that registers object types, simulations or disruption types; layouts load it by URL. Guide to plugins · Layout file format · Control-system interface

// plugins/windmill.js: a new object type
export default function (arail) {
  class Windmill extends arail.LayoutObject {
    static type = "windmill";
    static label = "Windmill";
    static params = [{ key: "height_m", label: "Height", type: "number", unit: "m", default: 18 }];
    computeGeometry() {
      const c = arail.resolvePoint(this.world.map, this.spec.position);
      return c && { center: c };
    }
    draw(view) {
      const [x, y] = this.geometry.center;
      const h = view.m(this.spec.height_m);   // metres → model mm
      view.prism(arail.rectFootprint([x, y], view.m(5), view.m(5)), 0, h,
                 { side: "#e8e1d0", top: "#8a5a3c" });
    }
  }
  arail.registry.registerObject(Windmill);
}

The digital sister

An image of the layout that can be laid over it, on any phone, live or projected. Technically independent of the layout: she simulates the lab next to it.

Stage 2Next

The layout writes into the model

Live positions from the lab's control system: physical trains in a virtual world. When a real train stops at a platform, the virtual passengers board it. The bridge runs with a simulator; the adapter for the EBL is next.

Stage 3Later

The sister runs operations

The sister takes over operations control and writes into the layout: disruptions hit the real trains too, flexibly and repeatably, for any exercise.
