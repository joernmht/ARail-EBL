The railway lab gets augmented reality

A digital big sister of the model railway, on any phone. Passengers on the platforms, a town with buses, a container terminal, drivers on their way to work, ageing infrastructure and a whole bouquet of disruptions, registered on the real layout.

Not only interlocking and train operation, not only the railway as a whole: the whole transport system, from the walk to work to the driver of a freight train that passes on containers between modes.

Each course switches on the functions it needs and develops its own scenarios. Everything runs in the browser: no installation, no calibration board.

1The base

A digital big sister of the lab

Digital twins are boring. We mapped the whole lab with ArUco markers and built a sister: an image of the layout that can be laid over it, with every phone, as a photo or a live picture, or projected. As a sister, she is technically independent of the layout.

  1. MarkSquare ArUco or AprilTag stickers, 30 mm, anywhere on the layout. Model wagons get small tags of their own.
  2. MapOne video of the table: arail-survey measures every marker and writes a fixed layout and an orthophoto.
  3. RegisterFrom the camera image alone, all visible markers give the camera pose and its focal length. Fixed camera positions are the steadiest for now.
  4. MirrorPlatforms, streets, houses, a terminal: a JSON layout in model millimetres, scaled to the prototype. A person on an H0 platform is 20 mm tall.
  5. Fly overPress F for a virtual camera: build on a grid on the orthophoto and add virtual table modules beyond the real table.

2Applications

What runs on the sister today

Five function prototypes, each one an example layout that opens in the app. Try them on the lab photo; they work the same on your own layout and on the live camera.

Prototype

1 · Passenger flows, town and buses

A town that goes to work

Passengers wait where their train stops and board through the doors; their colour shows their mood. Around the station, residents of Plattenbau, Gründerzeit blocks and houses go to work, school and shopping on foot, by bus and by train, on a fast clock through day and night.

In teaching: station and public transport planning, intermodal trips, demand over the day.

2 · Intermodal freight

A working container terminal

Container trains, trucks and barges, a gantry crane and a reach stacker. Pick a container and its new place, and the crane moves it. Model wagons with printed deck cards carry their containers on the real layout.

In teaching: freight transport, terminal operation, the hand-over between modes.

3 · Rail operations

Fleet, workshop and crews

Units that wear and fail, a workshop and the four functions of the entity in charge of maintenance; penalties down the chain of contracts. Drivers with rosters and working-time rules, who walk through the town to the depot.

In teaching: railway undertakings, maintenance (ECM), crew planning, stress tests such as a flu wave.

4 · Infrastructure

Asset management as a game

Track, switches, signals, interlockings and level crossings, each with a condition, faults and an age. Students play asset manager, dispatcher, planner and funding authority: maintenance in shifts, renewals through the HOAI phases with BIM, funding and tenders, year by year with limited money and people.

In teaching: infrastructure management, project planning, the condition you know and the one you don't.

5 · Disruptions and scenarios

A bouquet of disruptions

Delays, cancellations, closures, signal failures, crowd surges and rail replacement buses, started by hand or scripted as scenarios: rush hour with a signal failure, the crowd after the football match.

In teaching: dispatching, passenger information, exercises that can be repeated.

3Next steps

From a sister to a hybrid lab

The first step is done. Next, the sister learns where the real trains are; later, she can run the layout herself.

Stage 1Arrived

The digital sister

An image of the layout that can be laid over it, on any phone, live or projected. Technically independent of the layout: she simulates the lab next to it.

Stage 2Next

The layout writes into the model

Live positions from the lab's control system: physical trains in a virtual world. When a real train stops at a platform, the virtual passengers board it. The bridge runs with a simulator; the adapter for the EBL is next.

Stage 3Later

The sister runs operations

The sister takes over operations control and writes into the layout: disruptions hit the real trains too, flexibly and repeatably, for any exercise.

Bring it to your lab

One visit gives a fixed layout; the lab-session guide has the checklist. Any scale: H0, TT, N.

Print markersGetting started
  1. 1

    Print

    The marker sheets on matte sticker paper, or one per label on A4 label sheets (e.g. HERMA); check the scale bar with a ruler.

  2. 2

    Stick

    Marker 0 at a table corner, then one every 30–40 cm and at both ends of every platform.

  3. 3

    Film

    Slow passes along and across the table, two or three markers always in view: two to five minutes.

  4. 4

    Survey

    arail-survey writes a locked layout, a report and an orthophoto.

  5. 5

    Build

    In the flyover: table modules, platforms, streets, bus lines, houses; export the layout file.

  6. 6

    Connect

    arail-bridge with an adapter for your control system, or the built-in simulator.

For developers: plugins

No build step, no framework. A plugin is an ES module that registers object types, simulations or disruption types; layouts load it by URL. Guide to plugins · Layout file format · Control-system interface

// plugins/windmill.js: a new object type
export default function (arail) {
  class Windmill extends arail.LayoutObject {
    static type = "windmill";
    static label = "Windmill";
    static params = [{ key: "height_m", label: "Height", type: "number", unit: "m", default: 18 }];
    computeGeometry() {
      const c = arail.resolvePoint(this.world.map, this.spec.position);
      return c && { center: c };
    }
    draw(view) {
      const [x, y] = this.geometry.center;
      const h = view.m(this.spec.height_m);   // metres → model mm
      view.prism(arail.rectFootprint([x, y], view.m(5), view.m(5)), 0, h,
                 { side: "#e8e1d0", top: "#8a5a3c" });
    }
  }
  arail.registry.registerObject(Windmill);
}
