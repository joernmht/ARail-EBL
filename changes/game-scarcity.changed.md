- **Fault Night: every choice of the maintenance dispatcher gives something up** (`web/arail/game/maintenance.js`): a fault report says what is seen, not why. Each fault has a cause that only a team on site finds, with the trade that can repair it and the work it takes; before that the dispatcher sees how often it is which trade's, from experience. The two emergency teams are of two trades (signalling and track), and a technician of each is on call. With what a team finds, the dispatcher decides: a repair for good, a provisional one (faster, but it may fail again within the shift, and then the fault is back), guarding a danger until the right team comes, or leaving it. New faults (a suspected rail break, a gap in a fence with children at the track), a move to call a team off, the minutes a danger stood unguarded in the score. The computer's dispatcher plays by simple rules and no longer wins every night: it passes nearly every shift and can be beaten by judgment.
- **The signaller hears what the team found and plans** (the cause, a repair for good or a provisional one and how long), and **a provisional repair that does not hold fails the signal again**: the track is closed and opened a second time. In a session with a maintenance dispatcher the signaller's shift no longer ends with the last train while the failure is still open or only provisionally repaired. The lab seat takes the same steps.
