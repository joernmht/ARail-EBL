- **Running dynamics of a train** ([#84](https://github.com/joernmht/ARail-EBL/issues/84), `web/arail/rail/dynamics.js`): the forces on a train (the tractive effort–speed curve within the adhesion, Strahl's running resistance, gradients, Röckl's curve resistance, the rotating masses) and its run along a line, as fast as its traction and the limits allow, each limit until the end of the train has passed it, braking for lower limits and the stop. Tracks have line data: their length in the prototype (for tracks that stand for longer sections), gradient, curve radius, rails and the braking distance of the line. Tap a train: the info card shows its run to the next stop (beyond the layout where the tracks end) with charts of speed and time over distance, the minimum running time and the timetable's with its supplement; while the card is open, the track ahead of the train is drawn on the table in the colour of its expected speed, with where it reaches its top speed, brakes and stops. The vehicle catalogue has starting tractive efforts, driven axles, regenerative brake forces and auxiliary power.
- **Braking distances and train protection on the track ahead** ([#85](https://github.com/joernmht/ARail-EBL/issues/85), `web/arail/rail/protection.js`, `web/arail/rail/ahead.js`): the full braking distance by the Minden formula from the brake percentage and the gradient, drawn from the train's front, red where it reaches past the next main signal; the speed the line's braking distance allows (the braking table); PZB 90 with the train type (O, M, U), its monitoring curves after the 1000 Hz and 500 Hz magnets and a chart of a train approaching a distant signal showing caution (where PZB brakes it); the braking curves of ETCS and LZB (EBD, EBI, warning, permitted speed, release speed) to the end of the movement authority, also as a band. Signals have a kind (main, distant, both) and apply to trains along their rotation; balises can be PZB magnets of 1000, 500 or 2000 Hz.
- **Adhesion and the forces along a train** ([#86](https://github.com/joernmht/ARail-EBL/issues/86)): the adhesion after Curtius and Kniffler on dry, wet, leaf-covered and icy rails, and with sand, caps the tractive effort and the braking; **Slippery rails** (Interaction → Disruptions, `low-adhesion`) on one track of a platform make its trains come in and leave slower, as much as their running dynamics say; the coupler forces when the train starts on a gradient (on the strip of its vehicles too, against the 450 kN of a screw coupling), the run-in force when braking in G and in P, and the load limit (Grenzlast) of its traction vehicles.
- **Energy and energy-efficient driving** ([#87](https://github.com/joernmht/ARail-EBL/issues/87)): the energy of a run from the contact line (with what regenerative braking feeds back: all of it on AC lines, half on DC lines) or as diesel and CO₂, per train-km and per seat-km; coasting before the stop from the earliest point the running-time supplement allows, with what it saves, as a chart and as a hint on the track ahead (*coast from here*).
