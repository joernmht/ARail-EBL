- **Running dynamics of a train** ([#84](https://github.com/joernmht/ARail-EBL/issues/84), `web/arail/rail/dynamics.js`): the forces on a train (the tractive effort–speed curve within the adhesion, Strahl's running resistance, gradients, Röckl's curve resistance, the rotating masses) and its run along a line, as fast as its traction and the limits allow, each limit until the end of the train has passed it, braking for lower limits and the stop. Tracks have line data: their length in the prototype (for tracks that stand for longer sections), gradient, curve radius, rails and the braking distance of the line. Tap a train: the info card shows its run to the next stop (beyond the layout where the tracks end) with charts of speed and time over distance, the minimum running time and the timetable's with its supplement; while the card is open, the track ahead of the train is drawn on the table in the colour of its expected speed, with where it reaches its top speed, brakes and stops. The vehicle catalogue has starting tractive efforts, driven axles, regenerative brake forces and auxiliary power. A line of the rail operations with `"run_min": "auto"` gets its running times from the running dynamics of its train, with its stops on the way and the running-time supplement ([Operations](docs/operations.md)).
- **Braking distances and train protection on the track ahead** ([#85](https://github.com/joernmht/ARail-EBL/issues/85), `web/arail/rail/protection.js`, `web/arail/rail/ahead.js`): the full braking distance by the Minden formula from the brake percentage and the gradient, drawn from the train's front, red where it is longer than the line's braking distance (the card then says the train is too fast for its brakes); the speed the line's braking distance allows (the braking table); PZB 90 with the train type (O, M, U), its monitoring curves after the 1000 Hz and 500 Hz magnets and a chart of a train approaching a distant signal showing caution (where PZB brakes it); the braking curves of ETCS and LZB (EBD, EBI, warning, permitted speed, release speed) to an end of authority assumed at the train's next stop, also as a band. Signals have a kind (main, distant, both), belong to the track nearest to them and apply to trains along their rotation; balises can be PZB magnets of 1000, 500 or 2000 Hz, drawn ahead of trains that PZB supervises.
- **Adhesion and the forces along a train** ([#86](https://github.com/joernmht/ARail-EBL/issues/86)): the adhesion after Curtius and Kniffler on dry, wet, leaf-covered and icy rails, and with sand, caps the tractive effort and the braking; **Slippery rails** (Interaction → Disruptions, `low-adhesion`) on one track of a platform make its trains come in and leave slower, as much as their running dynamics say; the coupler forces when the train starts on a gradient (on the strip of its vehicles too, against the 450 kN of a screw coupling), the run-in force when braking in G and in P, and the load limit (Grenzlast) of its traction vehicles.
- **Energy and energy-efficient driving** ([#87](https://github.com/joernmht/ARail-EBL/issues/87)): the energy of a run from the contact line (with what regenerative braking feeds back: all of it on AC lines, half on DC lines) or as diesel and CO₂, per train-km and per seat-km; coasting before the stop from the earliest point the running-time supplement allows, with what it saves, as a chart and as a hint on the track ahead (*coast from here*); the driving styles compared (as fast as possible, coasting within 3, 5 and 10 % supplement). The rail operations count the energy of every trip from its units (a unit of the line's vehicle type: its `vehicles`, or the catalogue's vehicle type of its id; a line's `power`), and their comparisons show the energy from the contact line, the diesel and their cost; the setting `driving` (`fast`, `coasting`) compares driving styles over days.
