- **The signaller's shift is played on the table** ([Games](docs/games.md#the-shift-on-the-table); `web/game/table.js`, `web/game/live.js`, the play page): in a mission the picture behind a signaller's seat is the game itself, on the lab's layout of Bf Neustadt. The station of the panel lies on the layout along its real rails: the trains run there as cars and stop at the real platforms, a route is a band on the rails it locks, the signals are masts with their lamps, and the people of the layout's world get on the game's trains. Nothing is written on the table: a signal a train comes to or waits at pulses until its route is set, and the names and times are on the panel beside it; the table's controls are in the side bar. A click on a signal and then on one of the pulsing marks where its routes go sets the route; the panel stands small beside it and shows the same. The table is seen from above on the photo of the real table (the camera is the player's: parts of the station, drag, wheel, keys), on a photo of the lab, or through the camera of a phone in the lab, placed by the table's markers: augmented reality. **T** switches it; it is there wherever the live picture runs.
- `ServiceManager.feedArrived(dockId, {trainId, line, kind})` takes the registered vehicle (`kind`) that stands for a control system's train at a dock, for who draws its trains itself and says where their doors are.
- A level crossing asks the world's simulations whether a train of theirs is coming (`trainsNear(pos)`: true, false, or null where a simulation runs no trains): one that runs the line's trains itself closes the crossing for them, and the crossing no longer closes by the clock. The game's table answers so: the barriers of Brückenstraße close for the signaller's trains.
