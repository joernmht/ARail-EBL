- **The game engine: role games wired by flows, in turns and shifts** (`web/arail/game/`, [The game core](docs/game-core.md)): the game core gets **ports** (the flows a role's game takes from other roles and gives them: a fault report, a repair status, a track closure, a budget; `flows.js` holds 70 flows with their German terms, and the parties of the railway), **moves by name** (`Game.act`, kept so that a game can be played again), a **view** from which a page can draw a game it does not know, and **computer players** (`static player`). A **session** (`session.js`) seats several role games, each played by a person or by the computer, wires them by their ports and plays slow roles (days to years) in turns and fast roles (seconds to hours) in shifts on one clock; a turn's decisions reach the shift as state, the shift's reports the next turn. Flows travel at tick boundaries and the people's moves are recorded, so a session can be saved and played again (`Session.save`, `Session.replay`). **Missions are data** (`mission.js`, format `arail-mission/1`, `web/game/missions/`): seats, phases, rounds, conditions, injects, briefing and debrief; `validateMission` names what is wrong, `testRun` plays a mission with the computer on every seat.
- **The maintenance dispatcher's game, Fault Night** (`web/arail/game/maintenance.js`): a shift in the maintenance control centre of the district around Bf Neustadt: faults by their report paths, one or two emergency teams and a technician on call, the closure asked of the signaller before a team works in the track. With the signaller's game in one session (the mission `neustadt-morning`), the signaller's fault report sends the dispatcher's team, and the repair time is in another player's hands.
- **The signaller's game in a session**: its fault report, track closures, delays and the shift's account go out as flows; the repair team, a closure request and a request to hold a train for a connection come in; the condition of the assets decides whether the signal fails. A computer's signaller (`SignallerAutopilot`) plays the seat nobody takes.
- **The slow roles' games** (`web/arail/game/asset-manager.js`, `budget.js`): **Budget Years**, the asset manager of the district around Bf Neustadt (the maintenance strategy, the renewals, the emergency teams, now and then a proposal of the asset responsible; the year's faults, delay minutes, condition grade and money), and **The Night Session**, the budget committee's rail rapporteur (the ministry's draft under the ceiling, the auditors' hints, qualified blocks, a grant or equity for DB, the coalition partner; the year's account). In the mission `neustadt-years` they are played with the signaller and the maintenance dispatcher for three budget years: the budget is the district's money, the district's plan is the dispatcher's teams and the signal's chance to fail, and the morning's reports are the year's delay minutes.
- **The undertaking's and the transport authority's games** (`web/arail/game/transport-control.js`, `contract-manager.js`): **Transportleitung**, the dispatcher in the railway undertaking's operations control for the morning of the signaller's game (a feeder announced late: ask to hold the connecting train or let it go; passenger information for every delay; a driver who calls in sick, a unit with a defect, each with a lawful way out and a forbidden one; scored against play by the book and against the rule "never hold, cancel when in doubt"), and **Malus Month**, the transport authority's contract manager (the operator's incident list by classes: no service, no pay, and a penalty only in the operator's sphere; objections with and without evidence; the auditor; an operator whose finances fall under a harsh hand and whose quality slides under a lenient one). Both join `neustadt-years`, which now seats six roles; alone they are the missions `transportleitung` and `malus-month`. The signaller's game announces a late train two minutes before its planned time, and a train held for a connection waits up to the minutes asked beyond its own departure.
- **The lab as a seat** (`web/arail/game/lab.js`, the mission `lab-morning`): the operations of a shift played by the EBL's timetable simulation behind its HTTP API (the `ebl` repository, which now takes interventions). The lab's trains at Neustadt, a signal that fails in the lab, the station track closed for the maintenance dispatcher's team, trains held: what happens in the lab goes out as flows, and the flows of other roles become interventions in the lab. A game whose world is elsewhere has an asynchronous `sync()`, and the session's clock waits for it.
- **The play page** (`web/game/play/`): choose a mission and the seats (you or the computer), play its turns and shifts on one device, watch what the roles give each other, read the result and the debrief; a session is kept in the browser and can be resumed. The page draws every game from its `view()`, so a new role game needs no page of its own.
- **The role catalogue says who gives what to whom** (`web/game/roles/roles.json`, format `arail-roles/3`; [Roles of the railway](docs/roles.md), the roles page): every one of the 176 roles has its party (25 parties, from the Bund to passengers and residents), its scale and horizon (the rhythm of its routine decisions and how far they reach) and its `gives`: the flows it hands to other roles, each with what exactly is handed over and its receivers (about 1,000 entries, 2,900 flows from one role to another, in 70 kinds of flows); what a role takes is what the others give it (`takesOf`, `flowGraph`). `validateRoles` checks that every pair of roles that work with each other exchanges a flow, and a test that a game's ports are part of its role's flows. The roles page and the guide show a role's Gives and Takes; the guide lists the flows with their German terms and the parties with their roles.
