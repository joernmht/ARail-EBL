- **Faster live camera in the app** (`web/app/detection.js`, `web/live/detect-worker.js`): the markers of the live camera and of clips are found in a worker beside the page (the live page's), so the app draws and simulates while a frame is searched, and each new frame of the camera is searched once instead of in every frame of the screen. The frame goes to the worker as a bitmap, which the worker reads and sends back, so the page does not read the camera's pixels itself. The picture shown is the frame searched, with the pose found in it, so the layout and the picture stay together. Until the worker is ready, where a browser has none, and with `?worker=0` the markers are found on the page as before. In the test browser (Bf Neustadt, a 1280 × 720 camera) the app went from 10 to 21 frames per second; with the page's CPU slowed down 4 times (as for a phone; the worker is not slowed) a frame takes 164 instead of 250 ms. Before, the markers searched on a slow page made the processing size fall to 480 px, where most of them were lost.
- **Marker detection in half the time** (`web/arail/core/detector.js`): the grey image, the adaptive threshold and the contours of js-aruco2 are computed on typed arrays kept from frame to frame, to the bit as js-aruco2 computes them (the same markers and corners; `tests/js/detector.test.js` compares them). With the time saved the camera's frames are searched at a higher resolution, and more markers are found in them. The video survey and the live page are faster, too.
- **Streets, sleepers, shadows and the terminal's markings drawn faster** (`web/arail/objects/road.js`, `building-kit.js`, `track.js`, `web/arail/terminal/draw.js`): the pieces filled together in one path are no longer closed one by one (`fill()` closes them; in Chromium every `closePath()` took the longer the more the path held). The same pixels; on Bf Neustadt a frame is drawn in about a quarter less time.
