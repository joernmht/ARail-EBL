- **Playing a mission is a game, not a dashboard**: one picture fills the screen behind every screen of the play page, the place where the mission plays, where your seat works, or what a question is about (twenty close-ups: cut from the lab's photos, and taken in the flyover by day and at night with everything in view virtual). The missions are stops on a line, in the order to play them; the stop chosen shows its seats as faces to take, and Play. On the stage the game's figures are gauges, its picture (the signaller's panel, the dispatcher's district) the control desk, and somebody asks each decision, as in a visual novel: a portrait, a name, the question, the choices as large buttons (keys 1 to 9), one decision at a time, a deadline counting down, in a band across the bottom of the screen that lets the picture through a little. In a turn, what happened is read line by line (Space). A phase begins with its name over the whole screen. The tables, the score, the messages, the moves as forms, the flows between the roles and the full report are dialogs; the result is a verdict with stars and a question to take along. H leaves only the picture.
- **Placeholder portraits**: who asks is drawn as a silhouette (`web/game/cast/`: office, suit, high-visibility vest, uniform, headset) until there are pictures; `web/game/cast.js` says who asks in each game.
- **A live backdrop**: while a mission is played, the picture behind it can be the layout running (Live in the bar, or L): the world of Bf Neustadt with its trains, trams, buses and people, by day and at night, from a camera that flies to what a question is about. Loaded only when wanted; the still pictures stay where it is switched off, too slow, or less motion is asked for.
