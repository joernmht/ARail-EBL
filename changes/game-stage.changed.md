- **Playing a mission is a stage, not a dashboard**: the seat you play is a scene on the photo of the place where its role works, with the game's figures as gauges and its picture (the signaller's panel, the dispatcher's district) as the control desk. Somebody asks each decision, as in a visual novel: a portrait, a name, the question in a line, the choices as large buttons, one decision at a time. A phase begins with its name over the whole screen; the seats are the cast. The tables, the score, the messages, the moves as forms, the flows between the roles and the full report are dialogs, opened when wanted; the result is a verdict with the score and a question to take along. The mission chosen has the screen to itself: its seats as pictures, and Play. Long texts show their first sentence, the rest behind More.
- **Placeholder portraits**: who asks is drawn as a silhouette (`web/game/cast/`: office, suit, high-visibility vest, uniform, headset) until there are pictures; `web/game/cast.js` says who asks in each game.
