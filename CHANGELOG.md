# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and the project uses [semantic versioning](https://semver.org/).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **The app in German** (#120): View → Language (*English*, *Deutsch*), kept on the device, `?lang=de` for a link (e.g. the projector), else the browser's language; the app switches without reloading. German covers the page's frame, the HUD, the panels View, Settings, Build (with the settings of every object type) and Disruptions, the info cards (train data, railway systems, where a train may run) and the labels on the stage; numbers get the decimal comma, money and counts the language's format (`Intl`). A small translator without a build step (`Translator`, `web/arail/i18n/`): the English text is the key, entries with placeholders are patterns (`"{n} min late"`), plural forms by `Intl.PluralRules`, and what has no translation stays English and is noted. The framework stays English; plugins bring their texts with `registry.registerTexts`. Tests check the catalogues (placeholders kept, no text twice, the texts the app asks for, the page's markup) and that the German panels show no English but names. The tabs Terminal, Operations, Infrastructure and Journeys, the project page and the guides are still English. Guide with a glossary of railway terms: [Languages](docs/languages.md).
- **May this train run here?** (#133): every vehicle type of the catalogue has a capability profile (traction power and pantograph heads, train protection and ETCS level, train radio, track gauge, the countries it is authorised in). The info card of a train checks it against every track of the layout, with the reasons where it may not run (power, pantograph, train protection, radio, gauge, line category by axle and metre load, authorisation), and warns in red when it may not run on its own track; **Show where it may run** colours the tracks turquoise or red by it (View → Colour the tracks by). In the lab example's *Border station*, RE 1 may not run on the Czech track G3. For plugins: `checkSection`, `checkTracks`, `compatibilitySection`, `consistOfHit`. Guide: [Railway systems and trains](docs/rail-systems.md#may-this-train-run-here).
- **Vehicles, consists and the train data card** (#82): a catalogue of typical vehicles (BR 146, 185, 193, ČD 380, EU07, BR 218, ET 442 Talent 2, BR 642, DBpza and Bpmz coaches, Sgns, Eanos, Habbins and Zacns wagons) with length, masses, axles, brake weights per brake position (G, P, R, R+Mg), top speed and what they are equipped with. A layout's `consists` give the vehicles of its trains by line or train number (`vehicle_types` adds its own types; validated). The info card of a train shows its **train data**: the vehicles as a strip, length, mass, axles, top speed (and the vehicle that limits it), brake position and **brake percentage** (Bremshundertstel, with load-dependent and isolated brakes), against the one its track requires (`track.min_brake_percentage`), and the highest axle and metre loads. The lab example's RE 1, RB 33 and S-Bahn have consists. New registry extension points for plugins: `registerCard` (adds to info cards) and `registerCheck` (checks layout files).
- **Railway systems of the track sections** (#132): every track has its traction power, pantograph head, train protection (PZB, LZB, LS, SHP, …), ETCS level, signals, train radio, gauge, line category (EN 15528), loading gauge, line speed, country and infrastructure manager, named after the parameters of the EU Register of Infrastructure (RINF); what is left empty is the value usual in the section's country (DE, CZ, PL, AT, CH, FR, NL; typical values). **View → Show → Colour the tracks by** colours the tracks by one of them, over the camera image (on the real tracks) and in the flyover, with a legend, and marks where neighbouring sections differ; the info card of a track lists its systems. A **system change** board (Build → Infrastructure) marks a system separation section, a train control transition, a state border or the boundary between infrastructure managers, and says what happens there. The lab example has a module **Border station**: track G3 is Czech (3 kV DC, LS, Správa železnic). Guide: [Railway systems](docs/rail-systems.md).
- **Hover and click on everything** (#118): over the camera image and in the flyover, a tooltip names what the mouse points at and says what it is doing, and a tap opens an **info card** that stays up to date: people (where they live and work, what they wait for, their mood, their plan for today), trains and buses (stop, doors, delay; with rail operations the trip, units and driver), cars, the staff's vans and drones, containers and wagons, buildings (use, floors, people inside now), stops (people waiting, bus lines, next departures), streets, tracks, and the assets of the infrastructure (condition as it is known, age, interlocking). Related things are outlined (the stop of a train, the interlocking of a signal). **Follow** lets the flyover follow a person; in the flyover, Enter shows the card of what is at the cross in the middle (read out to screen readers); Esc closes it. Build and Terminal keep their taps. For plugins: `pickables(view)`, `card(hit)`, `describeObject`, `describeAgent`, `describeVehicle` on simulations, `card()` and `pickHeight()` on objects, `world.pick(view, pixel)` and `world.card(hit)` (`core/pick.js`, [Extending](docs/extending.md#pointing-at-what-a-simulation-draws)).

A module can be **exclusive** (`exclusive: true`: switching it on switches the others off, switching on another one switches it off) or **a layout of its own** (`"layout": "container-terminal.json"`: choosing it opens that layout alone; there the boxes show the modules of the layout it belongs to, so that clicking it again or another module goes back). - **Journeys** (simulation `journeys`, new **Journeys** tab, module *Journeys* of the lab example): an exercise for the railway operations lab. Instead of a town full of generated people, every attendee makes one traveller with a start and an aim (a building of the town or a station beyond the layout), a departure time and a transfer time, asks for **travel plans with transfers** (walks over the road network and through the underpass, bus lines directly or with a change, trains of the timetable directly or with a change at the station, with the time for each change; ranked by arrival, the fastest and the one with the fewest changes marked) and chooses one. The travellers then follow their plans on the layout: they wait at the stops, get on the bus of their line or a train of their line towards their station, ride out beyond the layout along the timetable or come in from there, follow a change of platforms, and take the next train of the line when they miss theirs or it is cancelled. Each has a number and a colour, its name over its head, a status (“Waiting for S 8 → Waldau 07:41 at Platform 2”), the planned and expected arrival and a log; **Show** follows it with the flyover's camera; **Results** compare planned and real arrivals (CSV). The travellers are kept with the layout; the module switches the town's residents and other passengers off (*Generated people too* brings them back). Without rail operations the journeys run the trains at the platforms to their timetable (the network in the format of the rail operations, or from the platforms' line names): trains come out of the sidings shortly before they leave, wait for people getting on, turn round or go to the sidings, and are held or cancelled by the disruptions at the platforms, with their departures on the boards; with rail operations the travellers take the operations' trains, with their delays and cancellations. New guide [Journeys](docs/journeys.md).

- **Container terminal** (simulation `terminal`, new **Terminal** tab): container trains, trucks and barges visit a terminal; rail-mounted gantry cranes and reach stackers move containers between them and the yard blocks, one move at a time per machine, with realistic speeds and lifting heights. Choose a container and its new place in the Terminal panel or by tapping on the stage (camera view and flyover; in the flyover also with the keyboard: Enter picks at the cross); call, unload, load and send away trains and barges (**Depart anyway** cancels their waiting moves; one a crane is working on leaves when the crane is done), send pickup and delivery trucks, save the current state as the layout's start state. Containers are 20, 40 or 45 ft (also high cube) with ISO 6346 check digits; stacking follows the span below; a container under another one is refused with the reason. - **Rolling-stock markers** (`markers.rolling`): model wagons carry tags of a marker family of their own (AprilTag 36h11, 20 mm, IDs of their own), one per container spot: tag ID = (wagon − 1) × 4 + spot. Any visible tag gives the pose of the whole wagon, lifted to the measured deck height; several tags are combined. Model wagons stand, move, are held for a moment when their tags are hidden and are lost after a few seconds; their containers stay on them. The validation reports a missing or unsuitable marker type for them, more `codes` than their family has IDs and wagon types with more container spots than IDs per wagon, and Build → Layout does not take their marker type for the layout.
- **Deck cards** for model wagons on the marker sheet page (`markers/?kind=rolling`): one card per wagon, as wide as a container, with a tag at each spot centre of the wagon type (Sgns, Lgns, Sggrss) in H0, TT or N, the A end marked. The page takes `type`, `wagons`, `stride`, `size` and `scale` from the address; the Terminal tab links to it once per wagon type with the layout's settings.

- `view.start` in the layout: `"flyover"` opens the layout in the flyover.
- **Rail operations** (simulation `operations`, new **Operations** tab): a railway undertaking runs the station's trains to a timetable out to stations beyond the layout and back, with **units** that have maintenance cycles (inspections by kilometres and days) and random **hard and soft failures** whose rate grows with age (a bathtub curve) and with the kilometres since the last maintenance. **Maintenance** follows the four functions of the entity in charge of maintenance (ECM): management, maintenance development (interval extensions), fleet maintenance management (the daily planning of workshop jobs and of the units for the rotations) and maintenance delivery (the workshop with its tracks, working hours, findings, parts and late releases); each function belongs to a party with its own working hours and response times, and messages between parties wait for them. **Contracts and penalties** (Pönalen): the transport authority fines what passengers notice (cancelled train-km, late and short trains, comfort defects), and the railway undertaking claims from the party responsible down the chain of contracts. **Crews**: duties built from the rotations under the German working-time rules, stand-by duties, contracts with weekly hours, a roster made the day before, sick calls, vacations and vacant positions, the way to work (people who live in the town walk to the depot's door on the layout; others drive or come in by train), and a dispatcher who covers sick calls with stand-by drivers or calls on free days, replaces late drivers and hands over duties when the rest time would be too short. The tracks of the station's platforms are run by the operations (docks in mode `plan`): their trains stand there with their units, driver and delay on the label, and the boards show their next departures. New guide [Rail operations](docs/operations.md).
- **Comparing setups** (Operations → Compare, and `node tools/ops-compare.mjs` on the command line): setups (integrated or distributed ECM, manufacturer full service, crew buffers, lean crew planning, or the layout's own `setups`) under stress tests (flu wave, staff shortage, heat wave, bus strike, slow workshop, a bad Monday, damaged units, or the layout's own `stress`), several runs each with the same random events: key figures, cancelled trains per day, net penalties per party, CSV. The staff is sized for normal times, so a stress test meets it.

## [0.1.0] – 2026-09-30

First open-source version, built from the "Bahnsteig-AR" prototype (Python + browser, German), which is kept in the git history.

### Added

- Web app (`web/app`): photo, video and live camera sources; editor for placing, moving and configuring objects; simulation, disruption and control-system panels; import/export of layouts; recording of the stage as video.
- Framework (`web/arail`) as ES modules without a build step, usable in the browser and in Node.js.
- Marker map with automatic survey: markers anywhere on the layout, positions measured from the images; one least-squares pose from all visible markers; focal length estimate; optional lens-distortion calibration.
- Marker detection on top of js-aruco2 with sub-pixel corners, centre-of-cell sampling and no dropping of nearby candidates (finds small, steeply viewed markers: 301 of 302 on the synthetic test path, 235 with js-aruco2's candidate filter); corner order matches OpenCV for all seven supported dictionaries.
- Object types: platform, bus terminal, building, tree, forest, landscape area, road, track, label. Plugin API for object types, simulations, disruption types and vehicle kinds; example plugins (windmill, road traffic).
- Services: timetable per platform track and bus bay, manual calls, arrival/departure animations of trains and buses.
- Passenger simulation (example) for all stop areas, with moods, boarding and alighting at doors.
- Disruptions: delay, cancellations, closure, rail replacement bus, crowd surge, signal failure; scripted scenarios.
- Control-system interface: feed protocol `arail-feed/1`, WebSocket client, in-browser simulated control system; Python bridge with simulator, replay and TCP JSON-lines adapters and an adapter template.
- Printable marker sheets with a scale check bar.
- Python tools: camera calibration with markers (JSON output for the app), synthetic test scenes.
- Tests (node:test, pytest, Playwright with accessibility checks), CI and GitHub Pages deployment; documentation.

### Changed compared with the prototype

- English throughout.
- One common layout frame for all objects instead of per-platform marker pairs.
- The Python desktop viewer was replaced by the browser app; calibration and synthetic scenes remain as Python tools.
